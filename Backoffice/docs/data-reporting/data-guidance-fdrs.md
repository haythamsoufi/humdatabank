# Data Guidance, FDRS

Use this guide when you need to understand what data and documents are required for Federation-wide Databank & Reporting System (FDRS) reporting, how indicators are defined, and how to fill in each section correctly.

Source: *FDRS User Guide 2024* (IFRC). An interactive version and additional resources are at [data.ifrc.org/fdrs/resources](https://data.ifrc.org/fdrs/resources).

## Overview

The FDRS is a Federation-wide repository of key National Society documents and data on a common set of important indicators, updated each year. Reporting to FDRS is a requirement of the IFRC Constitution.

Each National Society is the owner and gatekeeper of its own data and documents and is responsible for keeping them updated and complying with relevant national or regional data-protection legislation, with technical support from the Secretariat as needed.

### What National Societies submit

Each year, National Societies:

1. Receive launch communication from the IFRC Secretary General (typically in the first quarter).
2. Complete the FDRS questionnaire in the Backoffice — entering indicator data and uploading key documents (annual report, strategic plan, audited financial statement).
3. Respond to validation or clarification requests from the FDRS team if needed.

Data is triangulated and validated before publication in the [FDRS website](https://data.ifrc.org/fdrs) and the annual *Everyone Counts* report. National Societies can request updates after submission with justification, but accurate first submission is strongly preferred.

### FDRS and Unified Planning and Reporting (UPR)

Federation-wide data collection includes both FDRS and UPR. UPR has a more detailed scope and categorizes reporting by Host National Society, Participating National Society, and IFRC Secretariat.

Where UPR and FDRS overlap, some KPI values may be **prefilled** in the FDRS Backoffice. You can accept prefilled values or adjust them so they accurately reflect your data. See [Data Guidance, UPR](data-guidance-upr.md) for UPR-specific guidance.

## Key documents

In addition to indicator data, FDRS stores three documents submitted annually:

| Document | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Annual report** | Accountability and transparency document covering office bearers, branches, staff, volunteers, activities, people reached, disasters and responses, achievements, and goals for the coming year. |
| **Audited financial statement** | Complete financial information for the fiscal year: balance sheet, income statement, cash flow, and accountant/auditor reports. Must be externally audited by an independent certified public accountant with signatures from authorized officials. |
| **Strategic plan** | Vision, mission, period covered, alignment with Strategy 2030, strategic aims, planned actions, and projected budgets/resources. |
| **Picture of the Year** *(optional)* | A photo representing a highlight of the year for the NS profile and *Everyone Counts* report. Preferred formats: JPG, JPEG, PNG. Minimum 720×1080 px; recommended 1440×1800 px. Include a public description and credit. |

## Key definitions

### Indicator types

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Indicator** | The parameter used to report data. Each National Society reports one value per indicator per year. Official indicator names are capitalized (e.g., People Donating Blood). |
| **Key Performance Indicator (KPI)** | The eight main FDRS indicators: People Volunteering their Time, Paid Staff, People Donating Blood, People Trained in First Aid, Local Units, People Reached, Total Income, and Total Expenditure. |
| **Supporting Indicator** | Data that explains or comments on another indicator (e.g., currency for Income and Expenditure KPIs). |
| **Additional Indicator** | Stand-alone data that is not a KPI but provides important information (e.g., Governing Board membership). |
| **Disaggregated Indicator** | An indicator broken down by one or more disaggregators. Where a full disaggregation set is provided, the top-level value **must** equal the sum of disaggregated values (calculated automatically). If disaggregation is unavailable for a given indicator, report the top-level value directly. |

**Example (disaggregation):** For disaster response programmes, 265 people reached (Direct); 110 of those are female (Direct + female); 17 are male aged 70–79 (Direct + male + 70–79). If no indirect recipients are reported, 250 may appear under Indirect for the same programme type.

### Disaggregators for data on people

**Age** — Standard groups: 0–5, 6–12, 13–17, 18–29, 30–39, 40–49, 50–59, 60–69, 70–79, 80+, and Unknown. A person aged 39 years and 11 months falls in 30–39. Not all age groups apply to every indicator; the questionnaire shows the correct groups per indicator. If unable to disaggregate, report non-disaggregated data as Unknown.

**Sex** — Standard categories: Male / Female / Non-binary / Unknown. Non-binary includes people who identify as neither female nor male. In practice, National Societies often report gender identity as stated by the person. FDRS uses the label "Sex" for alignment with humanitarian sector standards; you may report on sex or gender depending on context.

### Supporting indicators for data on people

| Supporting indicator | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Covered by accident insurance** | People covered under a National Society accident insurance scheme (e.g., IFRC global scheme or equivalent) during NS activities in the reporting year. Disaggregated by age and sex. |
| **Deaths on duty** | People who died during the reporting year while performing duties for the reporting National Society. Disaggregated by age and sex. |
| **Availability of disaggregation by disability** | Whether disability disaggregation is available for the given indicator. |
| **Use of Washington Group questions** | Whether the Washington Group Short Set was used to disaggregate by disability. The six questions cover difficulty with: seeing; hearing; walking/climbing steps; remembering/concentrating; self-care; communicating. Answers: no difficulty; some difficulty; a lot of difficulty; cannot do at all. Use full implementation instructions from the [Washington Group website](https://www.washingtongroup-disability.com/). For children, use UNICEF/WG child functioning modules where possible. |

### Reporting scope

**Reporting National Society** — The National Society whose data is entered. Data covers all RCRC activities on the NS national territory, including work by Participating National Societies, IFRC Secretariat, autonomous Local Units, and (where applicable) Corporate Members. Each data point belongs to one reporting National Society and one Reporting Year.

**Exception:** Paid Staff contracted by the reporting National Society are counted regardless of where they work.

**Corporate members** — Organisational entities with some independence from the NS but meaningful, privileged links. For each corporate member, decide annually whether to include it in KPIs. Include if criteria such as the following are met (other similar factors may apply):

- Set up by the reporting National Society
- Uses the Red Cross or Red Crescent name/emblem correctly
- Guided by the Seven Fundamental Principles
- Financial statements consolidated in the NS statements
- Substantial participation in the NS governing body (e.g., majority, not a single seat)
- Shared procedures/systems with the NS (e.g., employment contracts)

Examples: blood donation centres, hospitals, ambulance services, youth clubs. The reporting NS makes the final inclusion decision — avoid inflating counts with irrelevant entities or excluding closely linked work.

**Reporting Year** — The calendar year the data refers to (e.g., 2024), unless an exceptional 12-month period applies. Financial data uses the reporting year that overlaps most with the NS financial year (e.g., April 2020–March 2021 → Reporting Year 2020; September 2020–August 2021 → Reporting Year 2021). Specify non-calendar financial years in the questionnaire or contact [fdrs@ifrc.org](mailto:fdrs@ifrc.org).

## Section 1 — Reporting National Society and reporting year

Confirm the reporting National Society and reporting year before entering data. Share the questionnaire with Local Units for collection and aggregation at headquarters where needed.

---

## Section 2 — Governing Board (Additional Indicator)

**Number of people on the National Society Governing Board (HQ)** — The highest national-level decision-making body between General Assembly sessions (also called National Council, Board of Directors, etc.). Includes President, Vice President(s), Treasurer, and other elected or appointed members. **Exclude** branch, section, or local governing boards.

- Disaggregated by: sex, age (including leadership profile for President and Secretary General)
- Supporting indicators: availability of disaggregation by disability; use of Washington Group questions

---

## Section 3 — Local Units and Branches (KPIs)

### Number of Local Units

Physical subdivisions of a National Society that coordinate and deliver services to people — branches, sections, chapters, headquarters, regional and intermediate offices.

**Counts as a Local Unit:**

- Units that directly provide services to people (including intermediate subdivisions that also work with communities)
- HQ or regional offices serving the local population
- Blood banks counted as corporate members of the NS
- Units of included corporate members that directly serve people
- Both a District Branch and a Town Branch if each works directly with local populations

**Does not count:**

- District offices that do not work directly with local populations
- Paper-only regional subdivisions without a physical office
- Independent corporate members not integrated in NS work

### Number of Branches

Organizational entities that serve as neutral community spaces for volunteering, humanitarian assistance, resilience, and local support. Roles and relationships with National HQ are defined in Statutes, with local decision-making through branch members, board, and volunteers.

**Branch types:**

- **Branch** — Second layer after National HQ; may be called Chapter, Hub, Unit, etc. Formal approval required to open/close. May include hospitals, blood centres, health posts, warehouses, community-based units.
- **Intermediary Branch** — Provides subsidiary services (specialized emergency response, regional fundraising, coordination). Supports Local Branches. Examples: Regional, State, Town, District, Provincial, or Supporting Branch.
- **Local Branch** — Closest to communities where most services are delivered; may rely entirely on volunteers (Sub-Branch or Community Branch).

**Does not count as a Branch:**

- **Community-based units** — Neighbourhood/village structures (service posts, links, volunteering groups) not defined in Statutes; often temporary or activity-bound; smallest organizational level.

## Section 4 — Financial indicators (KPIs)

Report consolidated Income and Expenditure for the whole reporting National Society (all Local Units and qualifying Corporate Members). Use accrual or cash basis consistent with your financial report.

**General rules:**

- Exclude internal transfers within the reporting NS
- Exclude in-kind flows unless included in your audited financial statement
- Exclude staff paid directly by others (treated as in-kind)
- Map financial year to the FDRS Reporting Year with the greatest overlap

**Supporting indicators:** reporting currency (ISO code); financial reporting start date; financial reporting end date.

### Total Income

Fiscal value of money, material goods, and services received during the Reporting Year from any source, excluding internal NS transfers.

Disaggregated by **source of income**:

| Source | Examples |
|---|---|
| Home Government | |
| Foreign Government | UK (DFID/FCO), US (USAID/OFDA), Sweden (SIDA), EU, etc. |
| Individuals | Public, membership fees, major donors, legacies |
| Corporations | Donations, joint promotions, sponsorship |
| Foundations | CAF, Lebara Foundation, Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation, etc. |
| UN and multilateral agencies | UNDP, World Bank, Global Fund, regional development banks |
| Pooled funds | START Fund, CHF, ERF (but **DREF → report under IFRC**, not pooled funds) |
| NGOs | Local and international civil society organizations |
| Service income | Blood, hospital, ambulance, search and rescue services |
| Income generating activity | Commercial first aid training, retail, product/service sales |
| Other National Society | Transfers from Partner NSs |
| IFRC | HQ, regional and country delegations |
| ICRC | |
| Other | Only when source is known; do not use "Other" for unknown sources |

Sales of products/services count as Service Income or Income Generating Activity, not Individuals/Corporations. If your classification cannot be mapped to these categories, leave source fields blank rather than forcing values into Other.

### Total Expenditure

Fiscal value of money, material goods, and services spent during the Reporting Year, excluding internal NS transfers. Not disaggregated.

## Section 5 — People Volunteering their Time (KPI)

People who voluntarily gave their time to support delivery of the reporting NS services for **at least four hours** during the Reporting Year. Includes youth programme participants. **Excludes** blood donors (reported separately).

- Disaggregated by: sex, age
- Supporting indicators: covered by accident insurance; deaths on duty; availability of disaggregation by disability; use of Washington Group questions

**Clarifications:**

- Count **people**, not hours (each person once if they volunteered ≥4 hours total)
- Report volunteers on national territory only
- Define by the 4-hour threshold rather than local "volunteer" or "member" terminology

---

## Section 6 — Paid Staff (KPI)

People contracted by the reporting National Society for a **minimum of three months in total** during the Reporting Year who are remunerated or are interns.

- Disaggregated by: sex, age
- Supporting indicators: covered by accident insurance; deaths on duty; availability of disaggregation by disability; use of Washington Group questions

**Clarifications:**

- Secretariat-contracted staff working in-country are **not** counted by the reporting NS
- NS-contracted staff are counted regardless of who pays them or where they work (in-country, delegates, out-of-country, staff-on-loan)
- Count a person once if contracted in non-consecutive periods totalling three months (e.g., first and last three months of the year)

---

## Section 7a — People Donating Blood (KPI)

People who donated blood at least once at a blood donation centre/bank owned or run by the reporting NS during the Reporting Year.

- Disaggregated by: sex, age
- Supporting indicators: availability of disaggregation by disability; use of Washington Group questions

**Clarifications:**

- Count **people**, not donations or volume (one person who donates four times = 1)
- Do not count people unable to donate for medical or other reasons
- If the NS promotes donation but has no facilities, count promotional reach under People Reached, not here
- Enter **0** if the service is not offered

---

## Section 7b — People Trained in First Aid (KPI)

People who completed at least one first aid course led or facilitated by the reporting NS (any level, online or face-to-face) during the Reporting Year.

- Disaggregated by: sex, age
- Supporting indicators: availability of disaggregation by disability; use of Washington Group questions

Report activities on national territory only. Enter **0** if not offered.

---

## Sections 8 & 9 — People Reached (KPI)

**People Reached** — People who receive tangible goods and/or activities offering protection and assistance — including positive change in knowledge, skills, awareness, attitudes, behaviour, and physical/psychosocial well-being — and who can be counted or reliably estimated.

Staff, members, and volunteers count only when they receive services for their **own** need or development, not as preparation to deliver services.

There is no single "People Reached" total. Two **independent** sets apply:

1. **Type of Programme** (2 indicators)
2. **Thematic Area** (9+ indicators)

The same person may appear on multiple thematic indicators in one year (e.g., long-term programmes + DRR + Shelter). **Do not add** indicators across sets or within a set — that would double-count.

- Disaggregated by: Type of Recipient (Direct / Indirect), sex, age
- Supporting indicators: availability of disaggregation by disability; use of Washington Group questions

### Direct vs indirect recipients

| Type | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Direct** | Reached at a delivery point (or through a reliable link, e.g., household head receiving goods for a family member) with the NS provider present to verify delivery. Delivery may be stationary (clinic) or mobile (household vaccinations). |
| **Indirect** | Does not meet the Direct definition. |

If someone receives both direct and indirect services in the same thematic area or programme type, report as **Direct only**. Each person counts at most once per indicator per reporting year, even with multiple services.

**Examples — Direct:** All household members counted when the head receives food at a distribution centre or participates in cash-for-work with reliable household linkage.

**Examples — Indirect:** Listener to an HIV awareness radio programme where audience size can be approximated; family members who credibly learn from students who attended an RCRC values session.

**Not counted:** Friends told informally about a radio programme with no systematic count; people benefiting only from macro-level system strengthening with no reliable recipient count.

### Common questions

- **"Beneficiaries"?** — "People reached" is neutral and reflects active engagement; impact is not assumed from counts alone.
- **Same as "people impacted"?** — No. Reach measures delivery, not quality or impact.
- **Same as "target population"?** — No. Actual reach can be above or below targets.
- **Households?** — Yes, if every member is covered and average household size is reliable (e.g., census). Apply direct/indirect rules accordingly.

Contact [fdrs@ifrc.org](mailto:fdrs@ifrc.org) if programmes do not fit any Type of Programme or Thematic Area indicator.

## Section 10 — People Reached by type of programme

### Disaster response and early recovery

Services to respond to and strengthen recovery from natural and man-made disasters.

Examples: emergency shelter; food and nutrition distribution; restoring family links; psychosocial support; any programme implemented specifically for disaster response and early recovery.

### Long-term services and development

Services to strengthen community resilience, improve health, and address needs of the most vulnerable.

Examples:

- **Health:** HIV/AIDS, malaria, TB, NCDs, MNCH, CBHFA, WASH promotion, hospital and ambulance services
- **Community development:** livelihoods, road safety, security
- **Social services:** psychosocial support, migrant services, elderly support, anti-violence advocacy

## Section 11 — People Reached by thematic area

Each thematic indicator stands alone. Count using the same Direct/Indirect rules as above.

### Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR)

Reducing disaster risks through managing causal factors — exposure, vulnerability, land/environment management, preparedness ([UNDRR definition](https://www.undrr.org/terminology)).

Examples: contingency planning; early warning systems; climate awareness; forecast-based financing; tree planting.

Count people only when they receive specific assistance or messages that can be counted or estimated; structural "coverage" of whole populations counts only with reliable recipient estimates.

### Shelter

Services before, during, and after disasters to meet accommodation needs, reduce settlement risks, and support recovery.

Examples: short/medium-term shelter assistance; longer-term housing, land and property, climate adaptation; technical support on safe design and building techniques.

### Livelihoods

Capabilities, assets, and activities for income and living — restoring and strengthening livelihoods without damaging the natural resource base.

Examples: infrastructure repair; social service restoration; basic needs assistance; vocational training; seed/husbandry programmes; community awareness on livelihoods.

### Health

Activities from promotion through prevention, diagnosis, treatment, rehabilitation, palliative, acute, and long-term care.

Examples: behaviour-change communication; home visits for vulnerable groups; facility-based care; vaccines and medicines; blood donation management; first aid training and delivery; MNCH; malaria nets; immunisation support; nutrition; outbreak response; surveillance; harm reduction; substance abuse services.

### Psychosocial and mental health services

Support to protect or promote mental health and psychosocial well-being.

Examples: psychological first aid and recreation; peer/group support; treatment for complicated distress; counselling in health facilities; specialized clinical care; torture survivor services; non-drug therapies in state systems.

### Immunisation services

People reached with life-saving vaccines for vaccine-preventable diseases.

Examples: routine immunisation support; supplementary activities and campaigns; site administration; risk communication; transport to sites; cold chain; direct vaccination; perception surveys.

### Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH)

Safe water, sanitation, and hygiene for vulnerable people.

Examples: safe water access; wastewater treatment knowledge; reducing open defecation; community management of facilities; vector control; hygiene/dignity kits; menstrual hygiene support; hygiene education in schools and communities.

### Migration

**Migrant and displaced persons reached with assistance and protection** — Support for people who leave or flee home seeking security (migrant workers, irregular migrants, refugees, unaccompanied minors, etc.).

Examples: food, water, essential goods, primary health care; integration support (legal papers, language, housing, employment); anti-xenophobia advocacy.

**Has your NS conducted migration and displacement needs assessment?** — Process to identify and analyse needs of migrants, displaced people, and others affected by migration/displacement.

**Has your NS integrated migration and displacement into strategic planning?** — Programmes account for migrant/displaced needs; services accessible irrespective of legal status; dedicated programming where applicable.

### Climate and environmental crisis

**People reached with activities to address rising climate risks** — Climate change adaptation, DRR, and anticipatory action reducing vulnerability to climate-related shocks and long-term change.

Examples: community DRR addressing climate risks; school/community climate education; mangrove restoration; early warning and evacuation plans; forecast-based financing; climate-induced displacement; vector-borne disease risk changes.

**People reached by heatwave risk reduction, preparedness, or response**

Examples: Heat Action Plans; heat safety campaigns; heat stress in first aid training; water distribution; heatwave information; check-ins with vulnerable populations.

**Has your NS implemented nature-based solutions?** — Protect, manage, and restore ecosystems for societal challenges and biodiversity ([IUCN Global Standard](https://www.iucn.org/theme/nature-based-solutions/resources/iucn-global-standard-nbs)). Examples: mangrove restoration; land restoration and sustainable agriculture.

**Has your NS implemented environmental or climate campaigns focused on behavior change?** — Campaigns promoting individual and societal behaviour to reduce emissions, adapt to climate impacts, or reduce pollution and ecosystem degradation.

### Cash transfers

**People Reached with Cash Transfer programming** — Cash or cash equivalents (vouchers), including cash-for-work, for essential food and non-food needs.

Examples: one-off post-disaster payments; repeated payments in protracted crisis; blanket or targeted provision; standalone or government-partner programmes.

### Protection, gender and inclusion (PGI)

**People Reached with protection, gender and inclusion programming** — Prevent, respond to, and mitigate violence, discrimination, and exclusion.

Examples: DAPS centres in camps or urban settings; SGBV and trafficking survivor support with case management, legal aid, referral pathways, shelters; safe spaces for women/girls or children; equitable access for marginalised groups; livelihood/life-skills for women and girls or migrants; public anti-discrimination campaigns (often Indirect reach).

Specialised programmes directly addressing violence, discrimination, or exclusion toward affected groups.

### RCRC educational programmes

**People Reached by RCRC educational programmes** — Safe, continuous, equitable access to inclusive, quality education.

Examples:

- **Access/continuity:** school construction, supplies, transport, limiting school use as shelter, youth clubs
- **Equity/inclusion:** institutions for marginalised groups, literacy/numeracy, language courses, catch-up classes, enrolment tracing, scholarships, school feeding, TVET, menstrual hygiene items in schools
- **Protection/safety:** MHPSS for learners and staff; temporary learning spaces in emergencies
- **Quality:** public learning sessions on RCRC topics; professional training (nursing, emergency brigades); teacher training

**Clarification:** Staff/volunteer training and capacity-building for RCRC personnel are **not** education programming for this indicator. See the [IFRC Strategic Framework on Education 2020–2030](https://www.ifrc.org/document/strategy-2030).
